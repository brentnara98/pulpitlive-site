How to use it

Set up once. Then it's one tap every Sunday.

1

Put the microphone near the preacher

The app can only be as accurate as what the laptop hears, so the microphone must be close to the preacher's mouth. Pick one:

  • Best: a wireless clip-on (lavalier) mic on the preacher, with its receiver plugged into the laptop's USB or mic jack.
  • Or place the laptop itself on the pulpit, within arm's reach of the preacher.
  • Or place the laptop right next to a church speaker, where the preacher's voice is loud and clear.

From the back of the hall, picking up the room, no app works well. The Listening screen shows a level bar and warns when the voice arrives too quietly.

Forgot the reference? Say “find” and the words

5

Preaching from slides? Keep them on the screen

Start your PowerPoint, Keynote or Canva presentation as usual; Pulpit Live listens behind it. Say a verse that is not in your slides and a small pill with the reference shows at the top right. Click it and the verse covers the slides; leave it and it goes away in a few seconds, so it never gets in the way when you read a verse that is already on your slide. Say another verse, or use Previous and Next. Press Esc, or the Exit button, and your presentation is back where you left it.

No slides? Turn this off in Settings and every verse goes on the projector by itself.

Hymns, with the music on the screen

Click Hymn. Type the number or a few letters of the title, pick from the A to Z list, or say “let's sing” and the title. The hymn shows as sheet music on a clean white page, with the words of the current verse under the notes.

  • “Next” or the arrow key turns the lines; after the last line the next verse begins.

Questions

Things pastors ask

Does it need internet during the service?

No. The speech engine, the Bible text and the license check all run on the laptop. Nothing is recorded and nothing is sent anywhere.

How accurate is it?

Very good when the microphone is near the preacher: a clip-on wireless mic plugged into the laptop, or the laptop on the pulpit. From the back of the hall no app works well. Words your preacher says differently can be added to a corrections list.

Can we use it with PowerPoint, Keynote or Canva?

Yes, and that is how the app starts. Your presentation keeps the screen and Pulpit Live listens behind it. A verse you say shows as a small pill with the reference, which goes away by itself if you ignore it; click it to put the verse over the slides, and press Esc to return to your presentation exactly where it was. Churches that do not use slides can turn this off, and every verse then goes on the projector by itself.

Does it show hymns?

Yes. The Hymn screen projects hymns as sheet music with the words under the notes, one to three lines at a time. Find a hymn by number, by title, from the A to Z list, or by saying “let's sing” and the title. The library is built into the app and grows with free updates.

Which Bible version?

King James Version only, all 31,102 verses.

Do we pay before or after the trial?

Either. Most churches try it for a Sunday or two first, then order. You can also order on day one. Paying never restarts anything; the key simply unlocks the app you already set up, with your settings kept.

How do we pay, and how do we get the key?

Press Pay with PayPal, pay by PayPal balance or card, and type the church name and your email in the note box at checkout. We match the payment and email the license key, normally within hours, always within a day. Paste it in Settings → License → Manage.

What happens after the 14 days?

The app locks and shows the purchase screen. Enter a lifetime license key and everything is back, with your settings kept.

Can we use it for Bible studies and home visits?

Yes. The Study style shows the verse before and after so a visitor can read along, and the laptop's own mic is fine with the preacher close by.

Windows says “Windows protected your PC”. Mac says it can't be opened.

The installers are not yet signed with paid certificates. Windows: click “More info → Run anyway”. Mac: right-click the app → Open → Open.
